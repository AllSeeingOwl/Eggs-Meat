##### **Design Note: Art & Visuals Modules ("The Fresco Fiasco" & "Picture This!")** 

This document consolidates the mechanics, reference materials, sabotage methods, and intellectual property workarounds for the Art & Visuals stealth learning modules. 

**1. Reference Integration and Trivia Banking** The "Famous and Infamous Artworks List" serves as the foundational trivia bank and cultural reference guide for the Art & Visuals modules. 

- **Trivia Foundation:** It provides the necessary historical data (artists, dates, and movements) for Eggheads to answer UI questions after completing jigsaw puzzles, such as identifying Leonardo da Vinci's Mona Lisa or Vincent van Gogh's The Starry Night. 

- **Mechanical Grounding:** It defines the real-world artistic techniques—such as chiaroscuro (highcontrast light and shadow) and sfumato (hazy outlines)—that are translated into physical gameplay mechanics. 

**2. Asymmetrical Setup: "Pick Your Poison"** Before the round begins, Meatheads utilise the "Pick Your Poison" pre-round system to dictate the arena's content and hazards to exploit the Eggheads' weaknesses. 

- **Content Selection:** Meatheads choose between the "Fine Art" gallery (classic paintings requiring cultural knowledge) or the "Pixel Art" gallery (video game characters requiring gaming knowledge). 

- **Environmental Disruption:** Meatheads select the arena hazard, choosing between "Strobe Lights" (violently flickering lights that obscure puzzle edges) or a "Wind Tunnel" (giant fans that blow Eggheads and puzzle pieces away). 

**3. Physical and UI Sabotage (Meathead Alterations)** Meatheads actively disrupt the Eggheads' attempts to reconstruct and identify the artwork through physical force and UI manipulation. 

- **Physical Destruction:** Meatheads can slide into the puzzle to snatch jigsaw pieces or "smear messy sauce" over giant floor paintings to hide vital features. 

- **Blind Bet Destruction:** Meatheads are given 60 seconds to completely destroy a finished painting by smashing tiles and smearing paint. Eggheads must gamble on dropping into the arena when the painting is 25%, 50%, or 80% destroyed, forcing them to identify the art from the remaining debris. 

- **Destructible UI:** Meatheads can physically jump up and punch specific words out of the trivia prompt (e.g., punching out "Mona Lisa"), forcing Eggheads to answer without context. 

- **Psychological Sabotage:** Meatheads can throw "Tantrums" to scramble the UI, activate "Nihilism" to completely hide all UI hints, or use "Sophistry" to display deceptive and incorrect hints to the Eggheads. 

**4. Visual and Atmospheric Filters** The modules borrow terms from art history and photography to alter how players physically perceive the arena. 

- **Sfumato:** A thick fog zone that completely hides Egghead movement from the Meatheads' UI. 

- **Vignetting:** The edges of the screen go dark, restricting the field of view and hiding Meatheads approaching from the sides. 

- **Monochrome:** A phase where the arena is forced into varying shades of a single colour, requiring players to only stand on tiles matching the current colour. 

- **Trompe l'Oeil:** Optical illusions that create fake visual 3D walls, which Eggheads can walk through to escape danger. 

**5. Copyright Mitigation & Mechanical Abstraction** To avoid potential intellectual property issues with specific artworks, the game abstracts fine art into "in-universe" mechanical systems and stylised UI. 

- **Abstracting Art into Geometry:** Instead of simply looking at a 1:1 reproduction of a copyrighted painting, Eggheads must interact with its structural composition. Players are tasked with locating the painting's Vanishing Point to fix its depth or targeting Chiaroscuro to restore volume. 

- **The "Rosetta Stone" UI:** The game bypasses the need for exact visual reproductions by heavily abstracting complex concepts into intuitive visual icons overlaid directly onto the gameplay tiles. This allows the developers to create unique, stylised artwork that still accurately teaches the foundational techniques of Renaissance and Baroque composition. 

##### **Design Note: "Silent Disco" & Classical Music Expansion** 

**1. Core Movement, Lighting, and "Destructible UI" Mechanics:** The "Silent Disco" module tests players' musical knowledge by translating audio into physical and visual gameplay. 

- **The Turntable Pacing:** Eggheads act as an "Orchestra" and must run on massive record players like treadmills to maintain the song's RPM. Their running speed dictates the pacing of the kinetic typography (moving lyrics) on the screen. 

- **Destructible UI:** Borrowing from the "Picture This!" module, Meatheads can climb the screens and physically punch out the song's title or key lyrics to hide clues from the Eggheads. 

- **Visualising Theory:** To bypass copyright for pop songs, the arena lights and objects pulse in time with the hidden music. This allows for the "Stealth Learning" of music theory: hazards might require rapid tapping to mimic Staccato (short, detached notes), or players might have to hold their position on a tile to survive a Fermata (a held note). Meatheads can counter this visual rhythm by deploying "Strobe Lights" to mask the beat or triggering "Bass Drops" that violently shake the floor. 

**2. Dual-Arena Bifurcation:** To prevent the song from completely stopping during Meathead sabotage, the module is split into two interconnected arenas based on the game's hardware detection system: 

- **The Lyric Arena (KBM):** Keyboard and mouse players handle "Transcription Tasks". They piece together the scrolling lyrics while Meatheads attempt "Signage Sabotage" to scramble the text. 

- **The Music Arena (Controller):** Controller players handle "Pattern/Rhythm Tasks". They run on the turntables and navigate the pulsing musical hazards. If Meatheads spin the record backward here, the beat distorts, but the KBM players can still try to read the lyrics. 

**3. Classical Music Integration (Public Domain & Instrumentation):** Because classical music from the 18th and 19th centuries is largely in the public domain, the game can play these sweeping orchestral tracks out loud without licensing issues—a strategy historically used by early animation studios. 

- **Solfège & Instruments:** Instead of pop lyrics, the Lyric Arena can display Solfège (syllables like Do, Re, Mi used to teach pitch) which KBM Eggheads must transcribe to guide the Controller players. Controller players jump on giant orchestral instruments (valves, strings, timpani) to keep the rhythm. 

- **Operatic Singers:** Eggheads must route the music to the correct classical vocal archetypes, identifying if a part requires a Soprano (highest female voice), Bass (lowest male voice) or if the singer is performing an emotional Aria versus a speech-like Recitative. 

**4. The "a.k.a." Trope System & Meathead Sabotage:** To ensure classical music remains accessible and fits the game's chaotic, slapstick tone, the game tests players on Contextual Memory Signifiers—the pop-culture tropes that made these unnamed masterpieces globally recognisable. 

- **Pick Your Poison:** During the pre-round strategic phase, Meatheads select the audio track based on pop-culture categories to target the Eggheads' demographic. 

- **The "Rosetta Stone" Translation:** When Eggheads successfully guess the pop-culture trope, the game's "Rosetta Stone" UI teaches them the actual academic title by displaying both side-by-side. 

##### **Pop Culture "a.k.a." Categories for Meathead Selection:** 

- **Animation & Cartoons:** Tracks like Rossini's William Tell Overture (a.k.a. The Lone Ranger or Horseback Chases) or Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2 (a.k.a. Bugs Bunny or Tom & Jerry piano duels). 

- **Gothic Horror:** Tracks like Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D Minor (a.k.a. Dracula or Haunted Mansions). 

- **Cinema & Sci-Fi:** Tracks like Johann Strauss II's The Blue Danube (a.k.a. 2001: A Space Odyssey or Zero-Gravity) or Wagner's Ride of the Valkyries (a.k.a. Apocalypse Now or Helicopter Assaults). 

- **Sports & Comedy:** Tracks like Puccini's Nessun Dorma (a.k.a. The 1990 World Cup Anthem) or Julius Fučík's Entrance of the Gladiators (a.k.a. The Circus Theme / Clowns). 

# **The Method** 

The flow of these modules is governed by a **"Master Design Loop"** that dynamically pits the cooperative problem-solving of the Eggheads against the physical disruption of the Meatheads. 

Matches are played in a **5-Level Gauntlet** featuring a 10:1 ratio (usually 40 Eggheads against 4 Meatheads). Before a match even begins, the game automatically detects your hardware, assigning Keyboard/Mouse players to "Transcription Tasks" (typing) and Controller players to "Pattern/Rhythm Tasks". 

Here is how the modules play out and how things change dynamically to create chaos: 

#### **The Core Loop: Phase 1 vs. Phase 2** 

- **Phase 1 (The Mental Objective):** The round begins with a "Stealth Learning" prompt (e.g., _"Find the Flying Buttress"_ ). Eggheads must swarm to solve it, requiring cooperation like "Stacking" to reach high terminals or using "Logic Lenses" to spot fakes. 

- **Phase 2 (The Physical Disruption):** Simultaneously, Meatheads earn "Heat" by causing chaos. They disrupt the module by charging through crowds, "Yeeting" scholars into pits, triggering "Tantrums" to scramble the Eggheads' UI, or destroying "Load-Bearing" environmental structures. 

#### **"Pick Your Poison": How the Modules Change Dynamically** 

To prevent modules from feeling static, Meatheads are given a pre-round strategic choice called **"Pick Your Poison"** . They act as the antagonists by dictating exactly how the module will change before the Eggheads spawn in: 

1. **Content Selection (Psychological Warfare):** Meatheads choose the trivia content to exploit player weaknesses. For example, if the Eggheads look like gamers, Meatheads might select "Ancient History" over "Pop Culture". 

2. **Environmental Disruption (Physical Warfare):** Meatheads choose the arena hazard, tailoring the physical map to their playstyle (e.g., choosing a slippery greased floor vs. deploying heavy obstacles). 

#### **How Specific Modules Play Out** 

##### **1. "Picture This!" (Art & Visuals)** 

- **How it plays out:** The round starts in a **Construction Phase** , where Eggheads must swarm to push heavy jigsaw pieces together to reveal an image (like a famous painting). Once built, the **Question Phase** begins, presenting trivia about the image. 

- **How it changes:** Meatheads can physically slide into the puzzle to scatter pieces, or punch specific words out of the trivia UI to remove context (e.g., punching out "Mona Lisa" so the question just reads "Who painted the [BLANK]?"). 

- **The "Blind Bet" Modifier:** Alternatively, Meatheads are given 60 seconds to completely destroy the finished painting. Eggheads are locked in a holding pen and must gamble on when to drop in: at 25%, 50%, or 80% destruction. Jumping into a heavily destroyed painting yields a higher reward, but it is much harder to identify the remaining debris. Meatheads can also trigger modifiers like "Strobe Lights" or a "Wind Tunnel" to make visibility and movement harder. 

##### **2. "Date This" (History & Timelines)** 

- **How it plays out:** Eggheads navigate a giant physical timeline, identifying the correct month or year of a historical event. Standing on the large "Month" panel grants base points, but Eggheads can risk standing on a much smaller "Date" button for bonus points, which leaves them highly vulnerable to physical attacks. 

- **How it changes:** Meatheads don't answer questions; they commit **"Signage Sabotage."** They physically drag, carry, or swap the heavy timeline markers. If a Meathead swaps the "June" and "July" signs, Eggheads might unknowingly run to the wrong safe zone. Meatheads can also modify the arena by greasing the floor to slide markers into Eggheads like bowling pins. 

##### **3. "Silent Disco" (Music Theory)** 

- **How it plays out:** Eggheads run on giant, treadmill-like record players to maintain the correct RPM. 

When spinning correctly, kinetic typography (moving lyrics) appears on the screen, allowing Eggheads to guess the song. 

- **How it changes:** Meatheads can physically grab the turntable to stop it in a tug-of-war, or spin the record backward, which dynamically causes the lyrics to appear in reverse on the screen (e.g., "Is... This... Life... Real"). Meatheads can also climb up the physical screen to punch out lyrics, or trigger a "Bass Drop" modifier that violently shakes the floor, knocking over anyone who isn't jumping. 

##### **4. "Equation Invasion" (Math & Logic)** 

- **How it plays out:** Eggheads are given a word problem (e.g., "Legs on a Spider minus Legs on an Ant") and must locate giant physical number and symbol blocks (8, -, 6, =, 2) to build the correct equation in an "Answer Zone". 

- **How it changes:** Meatheads can use their massive bulk to knock over the wall of numbers before it locks in or commit **"Symbol Sabotage"** by physically swapping the mathematical operators. If a Meathead swaps a "Minus" block for a "Plus" block, the Eggheads are forced to recalculate and rebuild the puzzle on the fly while dodging attacks. 

##### **5. "Egg-Cess Denied" (Linguistics & Typing)** 

- **How it plays out:** Eggheads are miniaturised inside a computer mainframe and must jump onto a floor-sized keyboard to type out passwords shown on a screen. 

- **How it changes:** Meatheads can **Re-Map** the entire floor, turning the keys into Morse Code or the NATO phonetic alphabet, forcing Eggheads to translate while they jump. Meatheads can also use giant mallets to permanently smash specific letter keys to force Eggheads into taking wider, more dangerous jumping routes, or fire "Pop-Up Ads" from cannons that knock Eggheads off the keys to reset their typing progress.
